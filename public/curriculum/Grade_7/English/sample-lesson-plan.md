## LESSON PROCEDURE

### A. INTRODUCTION (5 minutes)

**Teacher Activities:**
- Greets learners and calls the register
- Poses opening question: "When you read a story, how do you know what it's really about?"
- Shows a brief video clip or reads an engaging short paragraph
- Connects to previous lesson on reading strategies

**Learner Activities:**
- Respond to greetings and answer roll call
- Think-pair-share responses to the opening question
- Watch video clip or listen to paragraph
- Share what they remember from previous lesson

---

### B. MAIN ACTIVITY (28 minutes)

**Teacher Activities:**
- Explains what a main idea is (the most important point) versus supporting details (specific facts/examples)
- Models "think-aloud" strategy using a sample paragraph on chart paper
- Demonstrates how to use context clues to understand new vocabulary
- Distributes reading passages to groups
- Circulates to monitor group work and provide guidance
- Facilitates whole-class discussion of findings

**Learner Activities:**
- Listen attentively and take notes on main idea vs. details
- Observe teacher's think-aloud demonstration
- Work in groups of 4-5 to:
  * Read assigned passage together
  * Identify and underline the main idea
  * Highlight 3 supporting details
  * Circle 2 unfamiliar words and use context clues
- Complete graphic organizer with group findings
- Present one finding to the class
- Ask questions for clarification

---

### C. CONCLUSION (7 minutes)

**Teacher Activities:**
- Reviews key concepts: main idea, supporting details, context clues
- Asks learners to share one strategy they will use when reading
- Assigns homework: read a passage and identify main idea
- Gives a closing motivational statement about the importance of reading comprehension
- Previews next lesson on making inferences

**Learner Activities:**
- Summarize what they learned in their own words
- Share personal reflection on reading strategies
- Write down homework assignment
- Ask final questions
- Prepare materials for next subject

## DIFFERENTIATION

### For Gifted and Talented Learners
- Provide more complex passages at higher reading levels
- Ask to identify author's purpose and tone in addition to main idea
- Challenge to write their own paragraph with clear main idea and 3 supporting details
- Extension: analyze how main ideas connect across multiple paragraphs

### For Slow Learners
- Pre-teach key vocabulary before the lesson
- Provide shorter, simpler passages with clear main ideas
- Offer sentence starters: "The main idea is..."
- Allow use of highlighting tools and visual aids
- Provide one-on-one or small group support during independent work

### For Learners with Disabilities
- Provide audio version of reading passages for visually impaired learners
- Use enlarged print materials for learners with visual challenges
- Allow extra time for reading and completing activities
- Pair with supportive peer buddies
- Provide text-to-speech tools where available

## REFLECTION

**What worked well:**  
The think-aloud modeling was very effective. Learners were engaged during the video clip introduction. Group work allowed peer support and collaboration.

**What did not work:**  
Some groups finished much faster than others, creating wait time. A few learners dominated group discussions while others were passive.

**Follow-up:**  
Need to prepare extension activities for early finishers. Will implement more structured group roles (reader, recorder, presenter) to ensure equal participation. Will do a quick pre-assessment next time to better differentiate passage difficulty.
